# A Semester at Tsinghua: Four Months in Beijing
June 2025

I applied for the Tsinghua exchange almost on a whim during second year. Four months later I was landing at Beijing Capital International with two suitcases, zero Mandarin beyond "nǐ hǎo", and absolutely no idea what I was in for.

The campus itself is stunning — centuries-old garden architecture woven between modern lecture halls. The first morning I got genuinely lost trying to find a convenience store and ended up wandering past a lake and a pagoda before a kind student pointed me in the right direction.

Classes at SEM (School of Economics and Management) were intense. Professors expected you to speak, argue, and defend your reasoning in ways that felt closer to a courtroom than a classroom. My Global Strategy course had students from 22 countries — debates about market entry decisions turned into actual cultural flashpoints in the best possible way.

Outside of class, Beijing is just endlessly fascinating. I ate 47 different dumplings (I kept count). I saw the Great Wall at sunrise from Mutianyu — genuinely one of the most humbling things I've ever experienced. I took a sleeper train to Xi'an and walked the full city wall at dusk. I navigated the Beijing subway almost entirely through emojis and pointing.

The exchange taught me things no course could: how to feel uncomfortable and stay curious anyway, how much of communication is non-verbal, and how different "good software" looks depending on who you're building it for. WeChat Pay, Didi, Meituan — the Chinese tech ecosystem operates on assumptions that would turn Western product assumptions upside-down.

I came back to Vancouver with a much more honest sense of how big the world is, and how small my own frame of reference had been. 10/10 would recommend.
